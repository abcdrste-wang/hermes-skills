# TOEFL iBT 2026 — CEFR Descriptors by Section

Source: ETS TOEFL iBT Test Specifications 2026 (pages 7-14)

## Reading

**Basic (A1-A2)**
- Recognize familiar words and basic phrases in short, simple texts
- Apply basic spelling and word form knowledge to complete predictable gaps
- Recognize simple syntactic patterns
- Understand information in common, non-linear text formats
- Identify the main purpose of a short, written communication
- Recognize pronoun referents

**Independent (B1-B2)**
- Understand both prose and telegraphic language
- Understand moderately complex texts
- Use morphological and syntactic cues to infer missing words or phrases
- Understand informal language, including common idiomatic expressions
- Identify a writer's purpose within a correspondence
- Identify important supporting factual information
- Scan to pick out details
- Skim for main ideas

**Proficient (C1-C2)**
- Understand a wide range of longer texts on complex subjects requiring integration of lexical, morphological, and discourse-level knowledge
- Apply advanced syntactic and cohesive devices to ensure logical flow and stylistic appropriateness
- Connect information across a text
- Make inferences about what is implied, but not explicitly stated

### CEFR Scale (Reading)
| Level | Descriptor |
|-------|-----------|
| A1 | Understand very short, simple texts one phrase at a time; identify familiar words and basic information in everyday materials |
| A2 | Understand short, simple texts on familiar topics; follow basic instructions; use context to infer meaning |
| B1 | (implied: basic independent reading) |
| B2 | (implied: detailed comprehension of concrete and abstract topics) |
| C1 | (implied: complex academic texts with nuanced understanding) |

## Listening

**Basic (A1-A2)**
- Identify the main ideas and basic context of a short message
- Understand the important details in a short message
- Predict future actions based on what a speaker has said
- Recognize the purpose of a speaker's message in a short communication

**Independent (B1-B2)**
- Infer meaning from information that is not explicitly stated
- Recognize rhetorical structures (compare/contrast, cause and effect, etc.)

**Proficient (C1-C2)**
- Understand a wide range of vocabulary including idiomatic and colloquial expressions
- Understand the range of grammatical structures used by proficient speakers
- Interpret implied meaning and nuanced information beyond the explicit content

### CEFR Scale (Listening)
| Level | Descriptor |
|-------|-----------|
| B1 | Follow everyday conversations and public announcements when clearly spoken; understand explanations and presentations with visual support; may require occasional repetition but can grasp main ideas and key details |
| B2 | Understand detailed spoken input on both concrete and abstract topics at a natural pace; follow extended conversations and presentations, even in mildly noisy environments; recognize rhetorical structures and interpret idiomatic/colloquial expressions |
| C1 | Comprehend complex spoken texts, including academic talks and group discussions; extract specific information from distorted or low-quality audio; infer speaker attitudes, intentions, and implied meaning with minimal effort |
| C2 | Understand virtually all spoken language, including rapid, idiomatic, and colloquial speech; grasp subtle sociocultural and rhetorical nuances across a wide range of topics and speaker styles |

## Writing

**Basic (A1-A2)**
- Reconstruct a range of sentence structures (sentence-level)

**Independent (B1-B2)**
- Write reasonably clear and organized multi-sentence texts
- Write mostly relevant paragraph-length texts appropriate to an academic context

**Proficient (C1-C2)**
- Write coherent and well-organized, multi-sentence texts
- Write relevant and well-elaborated, paragraph-length texts appropriate to an academic context

### CEFR Scale (Writing)
| Level | Descriptor |
|-------|-----------|
| A1 | Write simple, isolated phrases and basic sentences; produce short expressions related to immediate needs |
| A2 | Write short, simple notes, messages, and personal letters expressing basic sentiments (thanks, apologies); convey straightforward information in familiar contexts using simple language |
| B1 | Write connected text on familiar topics or personal interests; describe experiences, impressions, and provide basic explanations or opinions; produce short essays or reports on topics of interest |
| B2 | Write clear, detailed text on a wide range of subjects related to personal or professional interests; express emotions and highlight significance of events in personal correspondence; present arguments and support points of view in essays or reports |
| C1 | Produce well-structured, coherent text expressing viewpoints at length; convey complex information and nuanced attitudes in formal and informal writing; write expositions on complex subjects, emphasizing key issues effectively |
| C2 | Write clear, smoothly flowing text in an appropriate style across a variety of genres; develop complex ideas logically and persuasively; produce critical reviews and essays with high grammatical accuracy and stylistic control |

## Speaking

**Basic (A1-A2)**
- (implied: basic sentence repetition and short responses)

**Independent (B1-B2)**
- (implied: clear responses with some elaboration)

**Proficient (C1-C2)**
- Respond to questions with clear, coherent elaboration
- Use accurate grammar, varied vocabulary, and intelligible prosody
- Produce text that is well-organized and coherent
- Use a broad range of complex grammatical structures

## Key Implication for Tutoring Design

Band 5 (B2-C1) means the test taker is solidly in the "Independent" (B1-B2) to "Proficient" (C1-C2) transition. Practice materials should:
- Include both B2-level and C1-level content
- Emphasize inference-making, rhetorical structure recognition
- Require paragraph-length academic writing with clear argumentation
- Build listening stamina for extended academic talks at natural pace
